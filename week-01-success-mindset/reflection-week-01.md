# Reflection – Week 01

**Author: Eze Favour**

---

## 1. Biggest Insight I Got About Myself This Week

I realised I have already been operating like a DevOps engineer for years, just without the cloud tools. Watching systems, catching failures early, documenting fixes, and communicating clearly under pressure is exactly what I did in customer service and branch administration. The tools are new — the instinct is not. This reframed my entire career story from "catching up" to "building on a foundation."

## 2. My Biggest Weakness or Loop I Noticed

I tend to let urgent client and CiroLearn requests eat into time I had blocked for deep, focused learning, then try to make it up late at night when I am already tired and less effective. The pattern is: urgent wins over important, then exhaustion wins over both.

## 3. One System I Implemented From This Week

Starting Monday, my DMI deep work block (6:00 AM to 7:30 AM) became non-negotiable and happens before I open any client or CiroLearn messages — no exceptions. This simple boundary has already changed how much I accomplish before the day's chaos begins.

## 4. What I Learned About Mindset and Systems

Discipline beats motivation every single time. A written, followed process outperforms a talented person working purely from memory. Organic proof convinces faster than paid promotion ever will. These aren't motivational quotes — they are truths I tested across years of customer operations and confirmed again this week.

## 5. My Version 2.0

Writing the "Version 2.0" article forced me to visualise where I want to be in 5 years — leading infrastructure reliability conversations, shipping Terraform-provisioned environments, contributing to open-source DevOps tooling, and running CiroLearn as a recognised talent placement pipeline. Putting it in writing, in past tense, as if it already happened, made it feel inevitable rather than aspirational.

## 6. My Week 01 Highlight

The highlight was publishing my Version 2.0 article on LinkedIn and watching people from both my old world (customer operations) and my new world (cloud engineering) engage with it. It proved that my story doesn't need an apology — it just needs to be told.
